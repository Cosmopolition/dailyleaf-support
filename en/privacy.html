Privacy Policy

OneDayLeaf does one thing: it helps you write today down as a single note, then keeps it on your own device.

Last updated: November 20, 2026 · Version 1.0.0

The official address of this policy: https://cosmopolition.github.io/dailyleaf-support/privacy.html

Support Privacy Policy

The short version: no account, no server, and no network activity except talking to the App Store when you buy the unlock or restore it on a new device. Your photos and words stay on this device.

The app integrates no analytics SDK, no crash reporting service, no ad SDK and no third-party tracking library. It also links no networking framework — that is not a promise, it is the absence of that code in the binary. What you write has nowhere to be sent, because there is no server to send it to.

Section 1 · What we collect

1.1 What we explicitly do not collect

Name, email address, account password, phone number, location, contacts, microphone, camera, clipboard contents, device identifiers, advertising identifiers (IDFA), crash logs, usage analytics — none of them.

We have no account system, so there is no such thing as "signing up". The app integrates no analytics SDK, no crash reporting service, no ad SDK and no third-party tracking library, and it does not request the advertising tracking permission.

1.2 What the app records on your own machine

So that you can write, save and find your notes again, the app keeps the following on this device:

  • Draft — the one you are writing right now: photo + text
  • Collection — every note you have kept: photo + text + date + the style used at the time
  • Trash — the ones you removed but have not emptied (a mistake can still be undone)
  • Preferences — interface language, paper colour, whether to show the date stamp
  • Whether all styles are unlocked — a flag remembered after the purchase

All of it is written to this device. There is no copy of it anywhere else.

1.3 What you hand over yourself

Only two things need you to do something by hand:

  • Pick a photo — using the system photo picker (PhotosPicker). It runs outside the app's sandbox, so the app cannot reach your whole library; it only gets the single photo you tapped.
  • Save a finished note to your Photos — the one and only time the app asks the system for permission. The prompt reads "Save the finished note to Photos".

On top of that, the app reads the capture time from the single photo you picked — it uses it to decide which day the signature should carry (make a leaf today from a photo taken last week, and the signature should say last week). Nothing else in the photo is read: no camera model, no GPS coordinates, no lens data, no edit history.

Section 2 · Permissions

2.1 One permission only

"Save the finished note to Photos" (system identifier NSPhotoLibraryAddUsageDescription). It is only triggered when you want to save a finished note into your library.
Reading photos needs no permission at all — because picking a photo goes through a system component: the selection screen runs on your device, and the app only receives the result.

2.2 What we never ask for

Camera, microphone, contacts, location, Bluetooth, background app refresh, tracking permission — none of them are requested.
Apart from the photo library write description above, the app's Info.plist contains no other permission declarations; you can download the app and check for yourself.

Section 3 · Where your data lives

3.1 All of it on this device

The app writes its files into the Documents directory of the app sandbox, and its preferences into the system preferences. Specifically, these:

PathWhat is inside
Documents/Archive/Your collection. Each note is a <id>.json (text, date, style) plus a <id>.jpg (photo)
Documents/Archive/.trash/The Trash. Removed notes that can still be put back
Documents/Draft/The one draft you have not saved yet
UserDefaultsInterface language, paper colour, date stamp switch, unlocked state
Temporary directoryTemporary files for PDF export and backup zip; deleted as soon as they are used

The app does not request NSUserTrackingUsageDescription, and it requests no background capabilities either.

3.2 The photos you keep carry no metadata at all

When a photo you picked is stored in your collection, it is re-encoded as JPEG, and that step drops the EXIF entirely — the file on disk contains no metadata of any kind.
In other words, a photo in your collection does not carry along the kind of information that says "this was taken here" or "this was shot on that camera".
The capture time was used once, to set the signature date. It was not stored.

Section 4 · The network

4.1 The app does not use the network

The app's code contains no URLSession, no URLRequest, and no request code for any http:// or https://. That is not a promise, it is something you can check: ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption is set to false in Info.plist, and no networking framework exists in the binary.

Therefore: no account, no server, no push notifications, no cloud sync, and no risk of the kind "our database was breached". The price is that there is no cloud recovery either — if you lose it, it is lost, unless you made a backup yourself.

4.2 The one exception: talking to the store

If you buy "Unlock all styles forever" (¥3, one time), or tap Restore Purchases to bring the unlock onto a new device, the app talks to the App Store through Apple's official StoreKit. The payment is handled by Apple; the app never touches your card details and does not know your Apple ID password.
Apart from that, the app makes no network requests.

This transaction is recorded in your own Apple account under Apple's policy — that is Apple's system, and we neither see nor control it.

Section 5 · Third parties

5.1 Everything is built into the system

  • Photos — the system picker helps you choose a photo, the system library helps you save one
  • StoreKit — appears only when you tap Buy

No third-party SDK, no third-party analytics, no third-party ads, no third-party font server. The four fonts bundled with the app are distributed under their own open source licences: three of them under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, and one free for commercial use by permission from its author — the licence notices can be read item by item under Settings → About this app.

Section 6 · iCloud backup (this is the system's behaviour)

6.1 We do not sync; the system backs up

The app has no CloudKit container and no iCloud sync feature — two devices do not keep each other up to date.

But if iCloud backup is on for your iPhone (Settings → your name → iCloud → iCloud Backup), then the Documents directory is backed up by the system along with the rest of the device. When you switch phones, those notes come back with the system backup. That is the default behaviour of iOS, not a sync feature we built.

If you would rather control the backup yourself: use Settings → Back up to Files, and the app will package your collection, Trash and draft into one zip (photos and text included) for you to keep; on another device, import it with Restore from a backup.

Section 7 · Exporting and sharing

7.1 Once a file leaves the device, it is your call

  • Export the whole book as PDF — you lay the notes you have collected out as one book and save it as a PDF.
  • Back up to Files — packed into a zip, and you decide where it goes.
  • Save to Photos — the finished note enters your system library, and after that it is the library's business.

Once you send one of these files out with Share, it has left your device. Whether the recipient keeps it, forwards it or deletes it is decided by you and them; we are no longer involved and we cannot see it.

Section 8 · Data security

8.1 We do not add encryption, and we say so plainly

The text and photos of your collection are stored in the app sandbox as ordinary files, with no extra layer of encryption. We chose plain files so that "you can open it and check it yourself at any time" holds true; the price is that it has nothing stronger than the system's own file protection.
Your first line of defence is the device passcode itself. Do not hand your unlocked device to someone else, and do not leave an unencrypted backup zip in a public place.

Section 9 · Children's privacy

9.1 Not aimed at children, and no children's data is collected

OneDayLeaf is not designed for children. Because we collect no personal information at all (children's personal information included), there is nothing extra to do here.

Section 10 · Your rights

10.1 Deleting the app deletes everything

Because there is no account and no server, uninstalling the app deletes every note, draft and item in the Trash, and nothing is left anywhere else. That is a good thing (it is clean) and also the price (there is no cloud recovery).
To keep something, back up to Files first. For any other way of disposing of it — removing a single note, or emptying just the Trash — the app has a matching action.

Your other rights over the data (access, correction, deletion, objection to processing, portability) hold here naturally: because the data is only in your hands, we have no copy we could refuse you.
